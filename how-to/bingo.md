## 1. What it is

A page of the GVR / RLV corner, `/gvr/bingo/` (Spanish `/es/gvr/bingo/`), that makes **bingo cards to print or
save as a PDF**. Each square asks the player to do something small that gets people talking: meet a GVR or RLV
from another group, name a favorite story from this month's issue, find someone who listens to the podcast, learn
when and where our Grapevine Meeting and committee meeting are. Players cross a square off, or write a first name
or an answer in it. A full row is BINGO.

It is for **engagement**: a light way to get people meeting each other and picking up Grapevine and La Viña, at a
home group, a district meeting, an assembly or an Area committee meeting (ACM), a workshop, our booth or table at an
event, or an online meeting.

- **Every game can be different.** Grids from 3×3 to 10×10, and rectangles (4×6, 3×10 …). Squares picked for the
  occasion, the magazine (Grapevine, La Viña or both) and the kinds of squares you want. Each card can be different
  from the others, or all of them the same.
- **A large pool of squares.** About 300 squares (295 in November 2026), every one in English and in Spanish. The
  committee keeps them in one file, `content/bingo/bingo.csv` ([section 6](#6-how-the-committee-changes-the-squares)).
- **Both languages.** The card's language is chosen apart from the page's: an English page can print Spanish
  cards, and the other way round.
- **Nothing to sign up for.** Anyone with a browser can make cards: no login, no account. Nothing is sent
  anywhere; the page only remembers your last settings in your own browser.
- **Up to date by itself.** The meeting squares and the strip at the bottom of a card take our meeting days and
  times and the committee's e-mail from the site's settings, so a changed meeting time reaches every new card.

The page has three parts: the **builder** (the settings on the left, a live preview of the real card on the
right; one column on a phone), a few **tips** on how to use it, and links to other pages for your service.

## 2. Quick start: cards for this weekend

1. Open `/gvr/bingo/`, or the GVR / RLV corner (`/gvr/`) → **Bingo for meetings and events** → **Make bingo
   cards**. (Searching the site for "bingo" finds it too.)
2. **Your game:** type the **Event name** (for example *Spring Assembly 2027*) and check the **Date of the event**.
3. **Grid:** keep **5×5**, or pick another size.
4. **Where are you taking it?:** pick the occasion, for example **Area assembly**.
5. **Cards:** set **How many cards** (10 to start with, up to 60).
6. Look at the **Preview** (on the right; under the settings on a phone): the arrows beside "Card 1 of 10" show
   each card.
7. Press **Print or save as PDF**, above the preview. In the print window, choose your printer, or **Save as PDF**
   to keep the file or send it to someone. (The line under the button says so too, and **More help with printing**
   below it covers the rest.)

That is all. Everything else has a sensible starting value. The page remembers your settings in this browser, so
the next time you open it they are still there.

## 3. Make your cards, step by step

Every change shows at once in the preview (typed words a moment after the last key). The preview is the real card,
at its real size, scaled down to fit: what you see is what prints.

On a phone, and on a laptop at a larger text size, the builder is one column and the preview comes after all the
settings: the **See the card and print it** links (at the top, under the grid's line and under the squares' line)
jump down to it. When the card is drawn small there, **Actual size** shows it at its paper size; slide it sideways
to read it.

### 3.1 Your game

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Card title** | The big line at the top of the card. Empty: "Grapevine & La Viña Bingo" (Spanish "Bingo de La Viña y Grapevine"). Up to 60 characters |
| **Event name** | Printed under the title of every card. A few English squares name it too ("Spot something grape-purple at Spring Assembly 2027"); empty, they say "this event". The Spanish squares say "aquí" or "hoy" instead (a typed name would need its article). Up to 60 characters. On a small card (2 or 4 on a sheet, or a big grid) a long title or event may print with a short date ("Nov 18, 2026"), or in smaller letters with a short date or none, so the squares stay at 6 points or more; when its letters get smaller, the builder says so under the box. A pasted link or e-mail address goes on two lines after a "/", "." or "@" when it needs to, and a very long word with no such place breaks where it must. The builder keeps a long word whole only when that makes the squares at most 10 % smaller and puts none of them under 6 points |
| **Date of the event** | Printed on the card. It starts at today (Central time). A few squares are offered only between certain dates (an anniversary, a meeting that has not started yet), so set the day of the event, not the day you print |
| **Card language** | English or Español. It starts at the page's language |
| **Magazine** | **Both**, **Grapevine** or **La Viña**. *Grapevine* gives the Grapevine squares and the squares about both magazines; *La Viña* the same for La Viña |

### 3.2 Grid

- **Grid size:** 3×3, 4×4, **5×5** (the start), 6×6, 7×7, 8×8, 9×9 or 10×10.
- **Custom:** any **Rows** from 3 to 10 and **Columns** from 3 to 10, so rectangular cards work too (4×6, 3×10 …).
- **Free square in the middle:** on to start with. It only exists when the rows and the columns are both odd
  (3×3, 5×5, 7×7, 5×7 …): on an even grid the switch shows off. **Words in the free square** are "FREE" ("LIBRE")
  with a small grapes mark; you can change them (up to 16 characters).
- The builder says how many squares each card has: "24 squares on each card, and the free one".
- On big grids (**7×7 and up**), and when the squares come out smaller than about 1.2 inches (a 6×6, or 2 or 4
  cards on a sheet), the squares show their **short words**, so they still fit. The builder measures the squares it
  draws to decide. Every square's words are measured and shrunk until they fit their box: nothing is ever cut off,
  and nothing on a card prints smaller than 6 points (to keep it so, the builder measures every square the cards
  could draw, and takes a shorter date or a smaller title first, then the meetings' shorter lines, when one would go
  under).
- When the squares come out smaller than about 1.15 inches, a line under the grid says so: **"Small squares leave
  little room to write — for names and phone numbers, choose 5×5 or smaller, one card a sheet."**

### 3.3 Where are you taking it?

**Anywhere** (the start), **Home group**, **District meeting**, **Area assembly**, **Area committee meeting**,
**Workshop**, **Our booth or table**, **Committee meeting** (our own) or **Online meeting**.

An occasion gives the squares that fit it, plus every square that fits everywhere. A home group gets different
squares from an assembly; an online meeting never gets a square that needs someone's initials on paper.

### 3.4 Squares

- **Kinds of squares:** eight chips, all on to start with, each with its number of squares — **Meet & connect**,
  **Read & discuss**, **Share your story**, **Podcasts, video & app**, **Carry the message**, **Meetings, events &
  contact**, **History & fun facts**, **Just for fun**. Tap a chip to leave that kind out.
- **Meetings & contact squares on each card** (0 to 4; **3** to start with): how many information squares a card
  gets. They tell when and where we meet and how to reach us, and they have nothing to write in: a tinted square
  with a calendar or mail icon, crossed off once read. Three of them are marked **Always**, so every card starts
  with all three: our **Grapevine Meeting**, our **committee meeting** and the committee's **e-mail address**.
  Squares marked Always count toward this number.
- **Choose squares yourself** (it opens when you tap it): every square, each kind of square a folded list of its
  own with its count. Open a kind to see its squares, or type in **Search the squares** (a search opens every kind
  with a match). Tick a square to use it, untick it to leave it out. **Always** puts it on every card; it shows
  ticked only on a square in use. **All** / **None** do a whole kind at once, and **Undo my choices** puts
  everything back. A square you tick yourself is used even if its kind, magazine or occasion is switched off; never
  in the other language, or outside its dates.
- A live line says what is left: "143 squares to draw from · 24 on each card".

**Not enough squares?** When there are fewer squares than a card needs, the live line says so ("Not enough squares:
27 to draw from, 100 needed on each card", read aloud by a screen reader), and the builder says **Not enough
squares for this grid** and what to do: choose a smaller grid, more kinds of squares, both magazines or
**Anywhere**. Printing waits until it fits; the browser's own Print (Ctrl+P) prints only that note.

On a small card (3×3, 4×4), three information squares take a big share of it. Set **Meetings & contact squares
on each card** to 1, or to 0: the **Meetings & contact** strip at the bottom of the card ([3.6](#36-on-each-card))
already gives both meetings and the e-mail address.

### 3.5 Cards and paper

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **How many cards** | 1 to 60 (10 to start with). The − and + buttons, or type the number |
| **Every card different** / **All cards the same** | Different (the start): each card gets its own squares, in its own order. The same: every card is card 1 |
| **Game code** | 4 to 6 letters or numbers (no I, O, 0 or 1), chosen at random the first time. It is printed on every card ("Game K7Q2"). The same code and the same settings make the same cards, on any device. **Shuffle again** draws a new code, and new cards |
| **Cards on each sheet** | 1; 2 on a sheet for grids up to 5×5; 4 on a sheet up to 4×4. Two or four small cards save paper; dashed lines show where to cut |
| **Paper** | **Letter** (the start) or **A4**. Pick the paper that is in the printer |
| **Orientation** | **Portrait** or **Landscape** |
| **Ink** | **Color**, or **Black & white (ink saver)**: no tints, for a black-and-white printer or to save ink |

Above the preview, under the print help, the builder says how many sheets it makes: "10 cards on 10 sheets of
Letter paper".

### 3.6 On each card

- **Name & group lines at the top (to draw a winner):** a "First name" and a "Home group" line at the top of the
  card, on to start with.
- **How to play:** a short box with the rules, on to start with. Our words: find people and do what the squares
  say; cross a square off, or write a first name or an answer in it; a full row across, down or corner to corner is
  BINGO (on a rectangular card, a whole row or a whole column); show your card to the GVR or RLV who gave it to
  you — words that fit a home group, an assembly or our table alike. **Your own words (leave empty to use ours)**
  lets you write your own (up to 320 characters). Small cards (2 or 4 on a sheet) print shorter rules; a text of
  your own longer than 140 characters prints as those short rules there, and the builder says so under the box.
- **Meetings & contact, with a QR code:** a strip with our Grapevine Meeting and our committee meeting, the
  committee's e-mail address, the site's address and a QR code to the site, on to start with. A whole card gives
  each meeting in full: "1st Wednesday of the month, 7 – 8 PM Central, Google Meet". On a smaller card the builder
  measures what fits, and takes "1st Wed · 7 PM · Google Meet" (also when the full lines would make the squares
  much smaller), or "1st Wed · 7 PM" when even that would put a square's words under 6 points; a small card leaves
  the site's address out of "Write to us" (its footer and the QR code give it).
- The write-in lines are labelled by the square ("Year", "Topic" …) or by its type: "Name & group", "Name &
  phone", "Answer", "Initials" (Spanish "Nombre/grupo", "Nombre y tel.", "Respuesta", "Iniciales": short enough
  for one line in the smallest squares).
- Every card also says, in small print, **"First names only — and only with their OK."** This line cannot be
  switched off.
- At the very bottom: "Card 3 of 10 · Game K7Q2" and the site's address.
- Nothing on a card prints smaller than 6 points: the squares' words, the labels, the headings and the small print.

### 3.7 Print or save as PDF

1. Press **Print or save as PDF**. With many cards the builder makes them a few at a time and counts: "Getting the
   cards ready… 20 of 60" (a few seconds; the page still answers meanwhile).
2. Your browser's print window opens. Only the cards print, one sheet per page, never the settings or the rest of
   the page.
3. Choose your printer, or, to keep a file or send the cards to someone, **Save as PDF** as the printer (the line
   under the button says so). **More help with printing**, under that line: on an iPhone or iPad, the **Share**
   button in the print options, then **Save to Files**. (On an Android phone, **Save as PDF** is in the list of
   printers, as on a computer.)
4. Leave the print window's own settings as they are: the paper size the builder chose, scale 100% ("Default" or
   "Actual size"), margins "Default". Each sheet carries its own margin, so Chrome and Edge have no room left for
   the date or the page's address. If another browser still prints them at the top or bottom, turn off **Headers
   and footers** (More help with printing says so too).

The browser's own **Print** (Ctrl+P, or ⌘P on a Mac) prints the cards too. Printing was checked in Chrome and
Edge. In another browser, look at the first sheet in the print preview before you print 60.

### 3.8 The link that makes the same cards

- **Copy a link to these settings** copies a link that holds every setting and the game code. Anyone who opens it
  gets the very same cards: the same squares in the same places. Send it to another GVR or RLV who will print at
  home, or keep it for next time. (If the committee changes the squares in between, some cards change too.)
- **Your last settings stay in this browser**: on a computer or phone, the page opens the way you left it (the date
  moves on to today once the event is past). The game code of a first visit is kept at once, so a reload gives the
  same cards. A private window forgets them when it closes.
- **Start over** asks first, under the buttons: **Yes, start over** or **Keep my settings** (Escape keeps them
  too). Yes forgets them and goes back to the starting settings, with a new game code and today's date. When
  nothing but the code differs from a fresh start, it does not ask.
- **Without JavaScript** the page shows a sample 5×5 card (game code GV65) that prints as it is, and says the
  builder needs JavaScript.

## 4. Which grid for which occasion

Ideas, not rules:

| Occasion | Grid | Where are you taking it? | Good to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home group, before the meeting or over coffee | 3×3 or 4×4 | **Home group** | 4 on a sheet saves paper; meetings & contact squares at 1, the strip on |
| District meeting | 4×4 or 5×5 | **District meeting** | people meet the GVRs and RLVs of other groups |
| Area assembly, ACM, workshop: at the breaks | 5×5 | **Area assembly**, **Area committee meeting**, **Workshop** | type the event's name; Name & group lines on, if there is a drawing |
| Our booth or table at an event | 3×3 or 4×4, a stack | **Our booth or table** | a finished card is a reason to come to the table and talk |
| Our committee meeting | 3×3 or 4×4 | **Committee meeting** | save as PDF and share the file |
| An online meeting | 3×3 or 4×4 | **Online meeting** | send the PDF before the meeting so people can print it, or play with pen and paper |
| An all-day event, or cards to take home | 6×6 or 7×7 | the event | bigger grids show the squares' short words |

## 5. Running a game: tips and AA principles

**Before.** Make a few more cards than you expect people. Bring pens. Put the magazines out where people can pick
them up: many squares send players to an issue, a story or a podcast. At a bilingual event, print some cards in
English and some in Spanish.

**Explain it in one minute.** Find people and do what the squares say. Cross a square off, or write a first name or
an answer in it. A full row is BINGO: show your card to the GVR or RLV who gave it to you. Nobody has to play.

**Anonymity comes first** (Traditions Eleven and Twelve):

- **First names only, and only with the person's OK.** The squares never ask for a last name, and nobody has to
  give a name at all.
- **Phone numbers only between people who want to trade them.** A "Name & phone" square is for two members who
  choose to swap numbers. The GVR and the committee never collect them. In an online meeting, never trade numbers
  in the shared chat: skip those squares (untick them in **Choose squares yourself**), or use a private message.
- **A filled card belongs to its player.** Hand it back, or let them keep it. Never keep a pile of filled cards,
  never post a picture of one, and never photograph the players. If there is a drawing, the "First name" and "Home
  group" lines are all it needs.
- **Welcome newcomers; never single them out.** The squares give to a newcomer (your phone number, in case they
  would like to call; a warm welcome) and never ask a newcomer for anything. A greeting is the other person's
  choice: a smile, a wave or a handshake.

**Prizes are not needed.** The fun is in meeting people and talking about the stories. If a group wants a small
prize, that is the group's choice (its group conscience): for example a back issue of Grapevine or La Viña from
the literature table. Bingo never involves money: the cards are free, nobody pays to play, and nobody is asked to
give money for prizes.

**Attraction rather than promotion.** The cards invite; they never press. Keep the game light and short, and stop
when people would rather talk. A square never ties Grapevine or La Viña to an outside cause, business or event.

**Service positions.** When a square leads to talk about being a GVR or RLV, call it a service position. Each
group chooses its own rep.

**After.** A square that asks for a story idea can lead to one in the magazine: Share your story (`/contribute/`)
says how to send it. Tell the committee what worked, and which squares you would add: write to
grapevine@neta65.org.

## 6. How the committee changes the squares

The squares are one file in the repository: **`content/bingo/bingo.csv`**, one row per square, every row in English
and Spanish. Changing it needs write access to the repository (the MKP715 login is enough). Making cards needs
nothing.

- **The file's guide is [content/bingo/README.md](../content/bingo/README.md)**, beside the file: every column, the
  six types of square, the placeholders, the categories and occasions, how to add a category, the AA rules the check
  enforces, and what a problem line means. Read it before a big edit.
- **Edit it on github.com** (open the file, the pencil icon, **Commit changes**), or in Excel, Google Sheets or
  LibreOffice, saved as **CSV UTF-8** (plain "CSV" loses ñ and é) and uploaded in place of the old one.
- **Meeting days and times, the e-mail address and the site's address are never typed into a square.** A square
  holds a placeholder that the build fills from the settings
  ([content/bingo/README.md → The placeholders](../content/bingo/README.md#the-placeholders)). To change a meeting's
  day or time, change the settings: the committee meeting in [Settings §3.2](settings.md#32-meeting--the-committee-meeting),
  our Grapevine Meeting in [Settings §3.3](settings.md#our-grapevine-meeting-grapevine-meeting).
- **Keep a square's `id` once chosen.** The builder remembers the squares a GVR ticked by hand, and a copied link
  names them, by their ids.
- **To take a square away**, write `no` in its `on` column (it stays in the file for later), or delete the row.

**What happens after you save.** The site rebuilds itself in about 3 minutes (up to about 10 more before every
visitor sees it). A GVR who has the page open gets the new squares when they reload it.

**The check.** Every save runs the workflow *Code check (tests and test build)* (GitHub → **Actions**). A red ✗
lists each problem with its row and the mistake, for example `bingo.csv row 14 (people-gvr-today): text_en: 95
characters (90 at most)`. **A broken row is left out of the cards; the rest still works.** The build's log lists
the rows it left out (`[bingo] 2 problem(s) — left out:`), and *Website update* still publishes: fix the mistake
soon. The check also makes sure the file keeps enough squares for a 10×10 card in each language and magazine, and
the three information squares marked Always. On a PC, `python tests/test_bingo_csv.py content/bingo/bingo.csv`
prints the same lines.

## 8. Troubleshooting

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Not enough squares for this grid**, and the print button waits | Choose a smaller grid, switch more kinds of squares on, pick **Anywhere**, or **Magazine: Both**. If you unticked many squares yourself: **Choose squares yourself** → **Undo my choices** |
| A square you expected is missing | Its kind is switched off, it does not fit the occasion or the magazine, its dates do not include the **Date of the event**, you unticked it, or it is not in the card's language. Or the committee switched it off, or it has a mistake (the Code check names it) |
| The printed card is cut off, or one card runs onto two pages | The builder's **Paper** must be the paper in the printer (Letter or A4). In the print window: scale 100% ("Default" / "Actual size"), margins "Default", no "Fit to page" |
| The page's address or the date prints at the top or bottom | In the print window, untick **Headers and footers** (Chrome and Edge have no room for them on the builder's sheets; another browser may) |
| "Small squares leave little room to write" under the grid | The squares are under about 1.15 inches. For names and phone numbers, choose 5×5 or smaller, one card on a sheet |
| "Your title or event is long for this card" under the Event box | On small cards (2 or 4 on a sheet, or a big grid), a long title or event prints in smaller letters, with a short date or none, so the squares stay at 6 points. Shorten it, or print fewer cards on a sheet (one a sheet: a smaller grid), to print it bigger |
| Your own **How to play** does not print on the small cards | Small cards (2 or 4 on a sheet) print the short rules when yours is longer than 140 characters (the line under the box says so). Shorten it, or print one card on a sheet |
| The tinted squares print white | **Ink** is set to **Black & white (ink saver)**; or, in the print window, tick **Background graphics** |
| A link from another GVR makes other cards than theirs | The committee changed the squares since the link was copied. One of you prints all the cards, or sends the other the PDF |
| A meeting's day or time on the cards is wrong | It comes from the settings: [Settings §3.2](settings.md#32-meeting--the-committee-meeting) and [§3.3](settings.md#our-grapevine-meeting-grapevine-meeting). After the change, reload the page |
| **The card builder couldn't start** | Part of the page did not load: **Reload the page** with a better connection |
| The builder opens with old settings you do not want | **Start over**, then **Yes, start over** |

**Tests to run** after a change:

```text
python tests/test_bingo_csv.py content/bingo/bingo.csv        # the CSV alone: its problem lines, if any
python -m unittest tests.test_bingo_csv tests.test_bingo_cards -v
# in a real browser, on a build as GitHub Pages gets it (tests/browser/__init__.py says how; about a minute):
GV_BROWSER_SITE=_site GV_BROWSER_CHANNEL=msedge python -m unittest discover -s tests/browser -t tests -p "test_bingo_cards.py" -v
```

- `tests/test_bingo_csv.py` (25 tests): the real file (no problem, enough squares, the meeting lines in the words
  the settings give), one fixture per kind of mistake (`tests/fixtures/bingo_csv/`), the *Twin* (the Python check and
  the build's JavaScript check give the same lines) and *Info* (the meeting lines, word for word, from fixed
  settings, each hour on one line with its AM / PM). Its `Real` tests judge the committee's file, so only the Code
  check runs them (`CONTENT_TESTS`,
  [Automation and troubleshooting §4.8](automation-and-troubleshooting.md#48-the-tests-before-publishing)).
- `tests/test_bingo_cards.py` (51 tests): the built pages in both languages and the cards' logic run in Node. Its
  builds use the fixture pool `tests/fixtures/bingo_csv/pool.csv` (48 squares copied from the real file and three
  whose words are markup, which must print as words), so the tests before publishing never judge the committee's
  file. Only its `RealContent` tests build from `content/bingo/bingo.csv` (enough squares, the e-mail, both
  meetings' lines, the three pinned squares, the sample card at 6 points or more), and they too are left to the Code
  check (`CONTENT_TESTS`).
- `tests/browser/test_bingo_browser.py` (16 tests): the builder in a real browser: every grid, the PDFs (portrait and
  landscape, 1, 2 and 4 on a sheet: their page counts, nothing under 6 points, nothing running over, no meeting time
  on two lines), a long typed event or title on four small cards a sheet (a pasted link too), a card of hand-picked
  squares from its link, the link, the card's language, the meetings strip (long, mid or short lines; the meeting
  place on a small card too), the limits (too few squares, black and white, the help lines, the remembered settings,
  Start over, 60 cards of 10×10), the phone layout, reduced motion, the page without JavaScript, and the corner's
  button.
